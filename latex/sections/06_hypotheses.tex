\section{Hypotheses}

Two primary hypotheses were formulated based on the dataset context, plus two
extended hypotheses that emerge from the severity definition.
Each is framed through task abstraction to map the analytical goal onto
appropriate visual idioms.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} c l X @{}}
  \toprule
  \textbf{ID} & \textbf{Type} & \textbf{Statement} \\ \midrule
  H1 & Primary
    & Bad weather conditions increase accident severity compared to
      normal conditions. \\[4pt]
  H2 & Primary
    & Rush hours increase accident severity compared to other times
      of day. \\[4pt]
  H3 & Extended
    & Road infrastructure with mandatory stopping controls (signals,

\subsection{H1 — Data and Task Abstraction}

\textbf{Data Abstraction:}
The dataset is a table where each row represents one accident.
Key attributes: \texttt{Weather\_Group} (categorical, 7 values),
\texttt{Severity\_Group} (binary: High/Low),
\texttt{High\_Sev\_Rate} (quantitative ratio).

\textbf{Task Abstraction:}
Compare the proportion of high-severity accidents (Severity 3--4) across
different weather groups.\\
\textit{Problem breakdown:} Compare $\rightarrow$ Category $\rightarrow$ Proportion.

\subsection{H2 — Data and Task Abstraction}

\textbf{Data Abstraction:}
Key attributes: \texttt{Hour} (ordinal, 0--23), \texttt{DayOfWeek} (categorical),
\texttt{High\_Severity\_Ratio} (quantitative).

\textbf{Task Abstraction:}
Analyse whether the hourly distribution of accident severity peaks during rush
hour windows (6--10 and 16--20).\\
\textit{Problem breakdown:} Analyse $\rightarrow$ Temporal Trend $\rightarrow$
Identify Pattern.
