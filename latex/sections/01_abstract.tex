\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

This report presents a data-driven visual analysis of the US Accidents dataset
(2016--2022), covering over 7.7 million accident records across 49 states.
Using a hypothesis-driven, visualisation-centric methodology, we examine two
primary hypotheses: (1) adverse weather conditions increase accident severity,
and (2) rush hours increase accident severity.

Critically, the dataset encodes severity as the \emph{impact on traffic flow}
rather than physical injury severity—a distinction that fundamentally reshapes
how insights must be interpreted.

The dataset was pre-processed with a $2\times$IQR outlier removal strategy on
\texttt{Duration} and \texttt{Distance(mi)}, and 2023 data was excluded
due to partial-year temporal skew, yielding 6.1 million clean records across
2016--2022.

Through Python-based visualisations backed by statistical tests, we validate,
challenge, and extend both hypotheses, and derive two new hypotheses that emerge
naturally from the severity definition itself.
